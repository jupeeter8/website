When I was a kid, anyone in their 20s seemed old. Old, as they have grown up. They are no longer a kid, unlike
myself. I wanted to be old like them. I thought it was cool to be older, that it brought freedom and I used
to think I'll have to live an eternity before I'll ever be 20. In retrospect I can't even remember what I wanted freedom
from. Since then a lot of time has gone by and most of it is something
I can not even remember, maybe because of my ignorance to the fact that I am growing older or my general lack of sense
of each year passing by, with most days being the same. I am 25 now and I had never given much thought to growing
older. For me, my days play out mostly in the same manner as any other, it's hard to tell that a year has
even gone by. I never feel excited about birthdays either at least not for my own. I have a few people in my life
who are important and so I think about celebrating their birthdays but beyond that, day after day, year after year
I keep getting older without thinking too much about it. I feel there will never be a point where I start looking
at my life with the annual lens like a lot of people do. I wish I could but tbh I don't care that much, yet a few weeks
ago I found myself thinking about the fact that I am actually 25 now. What a milestone. Or maybe not? Maybe it is both.
Humans have such a short life span that 25 years (or even 60 for that matter) feels like an insignificant amount of
time, a tiny blip in the history of everything. Yet my life is all I'll ever know and so when I am almost at the end
of the road, I think even I'll look back at this series of mostly similar days
(most of which I won't remember<sup id="ref-1"><a href="#note-1">[1]</a></sup>) and think about the interminability of living.
In that sense, being 25 does feel like a milestone. Navigating the mortal experience has
been fun and sad at times. I do not think about where I'll go from there. Sometimes I worry that I'll go nowhere and end
up nowhere. I do not know what I should do about that feeling, and that there are so many other things that
I feel I do not know.

### When will I know everything?
I have kept a journal for a few years now. My habit of writing for a lot of those years was erratic at best. 
Things that I had scrambled anxiously only when I was upset about something. Something big enough that I felt the need
to write a dramatic entry about it in my journal. Last year I read some of the earliest entries and I saw pages
covered in words describing moments of great sorrow, though now I feel less strongly about most of those, it is
impressive that I exclusively wrote about unfortunate events. Someone creepy enough to go through my journal would have
concluded that I am probably a sad sob. Of late my entries are more cheerful compared to when I started writing.
Sometimes I even write just for the sake of writing, thoughts that come to me and I write them down without any emotions
attached to them. Though not so soppy anymore, when I read my journal now I come across a new and a much bigger problem
that all of everything that I have written is riddled with three words "I don't know". Every time I write about my days
I write "I don't know", as in I don't know the answers to questions I ask myself. Why did I act a certain way? Why am I
upset? How did I feel about something? What do I like? What do I want to do next week, let alone next year?
All of these are answered with "I don't know". I gave it some thought and I do not like that I feel
I don't know the answers to these questions. I know at times these are difficult questions to answer but I feel it's not
right anymore to leave them at "I don't know" during the one time I sit down to ponder. Going into 26, I feel this
sense of urgency to try to know things. Not that my life will be derailed if I don't. I feel it will be much easier
for me to know the answers to questions I ask myself. That's one thing I have felt after being 25, not that people
really care.

My friends and family had asked me about my thoughts on turning 25. I had never thought about it nor do
I think about it at the moment. I had never thought of having milestones, like a stable job by when I am 25 or buying a
house or travelling the world. I mostly navigate life as it happens, there are no hard requirements that I have made for
myself. Being 25 feels the same as being 24 or 16. I think at 16 I had a long list of things I wanted to
do. I am not sure but I vaguely remember having unreachable goals. The only difference is that now I am 25, maybe I have
the means and the freedom to achieve those unreachable goals. I guess that was the freedom I aspired for
when I was a kid. My milestone for 26 should be
to do more things that I think are unreachable<sup id="ref-2"><a href="#note-2">[2]</a></sup>. Regardless, I tried to write this blog as a reflection of things I had
done so far as an answer to being asked how I feel about being 25, after giving it some thought I realised that I don't
have much to reflect on, if any, nor do I care to write about it. Instead I will write about how funny<sup id="ref-3"><a href="#note-3">[3]</a></sup> life in general
is.

### Keeping friends is funny because it's strange
In the last few weeks I thought about my friends briefly. A thought in passing that as my days continue at my new job
I get to see less of them. I was thinking how now I spend my time with a completely new set of people, maybe my friends
do too.
I am not sure about that but for me, 3 people I regularly hung out with are no longer there, for them maybe it does not
matter, unless one of them also finds a new job or leaves the city. I wondered if that happened would they think
of the same things as I think of now. Or is this the cycle of life from here on, that my twenties will be a series of
people who come and go, some will stick and most will be just people that I remember spending time with. I don't mean
to say that people who become an artifact of my memory were somehow less important. If I remember
spending time with someone then they were important for me. I just find it funny that one day I'll stop
seeing people I consider important right now and never see them again. One day they will come and tell
me "Anirudh, I am moving to Brazil" and then I'll never see them again, maybe on Instagram doing
some stereotypical Brazilian thing. It does not
bother me one bit that it will happen. I just think about it from time to time. I spend some time with new people now
and at times I have my reservations about them. I think I worry more about whether I'll ever find the kind of
people I have had until this point (before I turned 25) or worse would I compare everyone new I meet so much to my old
friends that my judgement will always be skewed.
All of these thoughts and the possibility of one thing over the other is funny.

### Asking others to do things for you is funny because it's stupid
Another aspect of being slightly grown up that I find laughable is the painstaking ordeal I have to go through to get
anything done. I have to jump through so many hoops for things that I imagined would be much simpler. Doing laundry
and getting groceries is easy. I get it done the way I want and when I want. Tasks that begin to involve others for
something very simple feels like a joke. I feel like I am a character from a comic and my entire shtick is being
unaware of how to get others to do something very trivial for me. These so called "others" are purpose made to stop me
on the tracks. "Oh Anirudh, I see you want to get your bike repaired because you had an accident, here is a 50 step
list". I look at those steps and they make no sense. I have not heard of most of the things on the list I have been
handed. I don't even understand the reason behind some of them. Why do you need a picture of me on the
spot where I had my accident? Is this a
test to see if I feel traumatized standing on the place where I almost died in order to determine if I'm lying about the
reason behind why my bike is in three pieces instead of one? Who is going to look at that picture? What are your
encryption standards and are you SOC 2 compliant? Why am I even asking those questions? He is probably
going to send it to someone on WhatsApp and then even zucc will know what happened to me. Once I
complete those 50 steps I get to talk to someone who will give me a list of another 50 things I
must now complete and the cycle repeats. Most of the list is
illegible, some things don't make any sense. I hear American sitcom laugh tracks playing every time I am told
that there are more levels to this shit.
I am completing feeder missions of a life simulator game and when I reach the final
boss they will ask me to do something so absurd that I will actually say "This has to be a joke". I don't get it and
it is very annoying but I can't help but think that this entire process of getting anything done now that I am 25 is
hilarious and herculean at the same time. Like man I just want my bike back. Why can't you pick up your hammer and
strike it twice. That should do it. Where did all this paper work come from. How did people get their bikes repaired
before the invention of printer and WhatsApp. Fuck you. No respect.

One might think how is any of this funny. I believe I am talking mostly about control this entire time.
I expect things around me to work in the way I want. I expect to have a say in who I am friends with
yet as things happen I feel the rein of control keeps slipping from my hand. My attempts to keep it
together are funny. I guess either it's futile to try or I am yet to learn a thing or two.
